Business Rules and Relationships - Questions

  1. Look at these two statements:

    "An employee manages a store."
    "An employee works at a store."

    Could these two relationships have different types even though they involve the same entities? Explain, and give the likely relationship type for each.

  2. Write your own business rules. Choose a real setting you know well (a canteen, a jeepney route, a barangay clinic, a school club).

    Write:

    • one business rule that creates a 1:M relationship,
    • one that creates an M:N relationship,
    • one that is a constraint (a limit or restriction).

  3. A designer named the entities in a school system TBL1, Thing, StudInfo_X and Person2. Using the lesson's guidance on naming conventions, explain what is wrong with these names and suggest better ones.

  4. A shop owner says, "Each order is paid in one payment." The cashier says, "Customers sometimes pay half now and half on pickup." As the database designer, how would you reconcile these two views? What business rule would you write, and what relationship type would result between Order and Payment?

  5. "Business rules are the job of managers, not database designers." Do you agree or disagree? Support your answer using ideas from the lesson.

  6. A small online bookstore has customers who place orders. Each order can contain several books, and a single book title can appear in many orders. Each order has one delivery.

    1. List the entities.
    2. Write the business rules in sentence form.
    3. Identify each relationship type.
    4. Point out any relationship that might need an extra entity to handle it properly, and explain why.
